### A note for professional collaboration

I share this to explain the values and experiences that shape how I approach collaboration and research today, especially if my background or choices ever seem unusual or hard to interpret from the outside. My collaborations work best when they're grounded in mutual respect, intellectual honesty, and clear boundaries. I aim to work with people who value aligned goals over superficial metrics or pressure to conform.

During my 11+ years in the USA, I have been fortunate to find good friends, encouraging long‑term mentors, and deeply trustworthy people's support I had never experienced before in my professional life elsewhere— and I deeply respect those who supported, encouraged, and stood by me. This support is what helped me move forward. In contrast, in my earlier professional experiences in India, I felt that a woman's intelligence was valued far less than her appearance, that real support was rare, and even basic respect could take decades. Considering my upbringing across both Indian and Christian values, I found the USA to be far more culturally advanced in how it recognizes and respects intellect. I was also part of circles that read different mythologies and scriptures, which, together with my family’s literary interests, brought me into contact with people from many cultures and religious backgrounds. I did not come to the USA to become rich or to obtain citizenship via shortcuts, but to seek an intellectually and physically safe, mutually respectful place where thinking and curiosity are taken seriously and respected, while genuine hard work is recognized, rewarded, and appreciated, over mere appearances—  whether framed as "beauty with brains" or simply "brains" however this may be perceived. Because of my multicultural, enriching upbringing, unique childhood, and aligned early‑life experiences, I have often been drawn to rare, unique friendships and collaborations that feel intellectually well‑aligned, rather than fitting stereotyped expectations or a one‑judgment‑fits‑all point of view. Any questions beyond this context‑sensitive information may be discussed further, out of respect for thoughtful and respectful inquiry.

Given recent events in which some individuals have misread these context‑sensitive, unique life experiences and this upbringing, and have consistently tried to coerce me into collaborating or aligning with people who do not share my values and intellect, I felt it necessary to clarify them explicitly. This has deeply hurt me and has also shown me how to draw boundaries for respectful collaborations while allowing myself to be a bit more vulnerable and open about myself, so that communication can remain enriching, intellectually reflective, and respectful, even when it feels overwhelming to me. I sometimes struggle to know whether to feel sad about these coercive tactics and the lack of respect for my boundaries, or to see them as a sign that people recognize the value and potential of my work— whether they focus only on my work's earning potential or on true value alignment. I also try to respond responsibly, respectfully, empathetically, and proactively, (even when this is not reciprocated) and I recognize that the cognitive dissonance of "whom to trust" in the presence of misinformation is real, and that I am not alone in experiencing it. I have gradually and permanently distanced myself from misaligned experiences by seeking strong support systems in the USA and at the university level at various times, and I am truly grateful for these. **I look forward to collaborations with long‑term friends, long‑term mentors, and new colleagues or new mentors as well and to meeting more people with whom I can pursue common research goals and value‑aligned work.** I try not to fall into people‑pleasing; I prefer to build collaborations that are honest about my values and grounded in a genuine openness to meet and understand others.
